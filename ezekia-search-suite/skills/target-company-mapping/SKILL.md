# Target Company Mapping

Required: client name, role, and location. Ask for these if not given —
reuse them from a position-spec brief already in the conversation rather
than re-asking.

Do a web search and create a list of 5 each of T1, T2, and T3 target
companies for the project. The definition of the tiers:

**Tier 1 (T1)** — the closest, most direct fit. Match the client's sector,
size, geography, business model, and culture almost exactly; typically
includes direct competitors. Prioritize these for initial outreach — the
highest-probability source of qualified, relevant candidates.

**Tier 2 (T2)** — a strong but slightly looser fit. Adjacent sector, a
different but comparable size bracket, or a related market. Still credible
sources of talent; broadens the pool without straying too far from the
brief, and sometimes surfaces companies the client hasn't thought of.

**Tier 3 (T3)** — more peripheral or aspirational. Different industry
entirely but with transferable skill sets, smaller/larger than ideal, or
adjacent geographies. Used to round out a longlist, test for unconventional
but compelling candidates, or as a fallback if T1/T2 don't yield enough.

## If a database-mining pass already ran

Check whether any current or former employers of people already surfaced in
this conversation (from a database-mining pass) should inform the list —
a company where the firm already has strong relationships or several known
candidates is worth including even if it wouldn't otherwise rank highly.
Don't let this override the tiering logic, just let it inform tier
placement.

## Output

Visualize the results in a table: company name, city/country location,
tier, and a short sentence on why it's a match.

## Not this skill's job

Finding candidates at these companies (executive-mapping), or checking them
against the Ezekia database (database-mining, which should run first).
