# Comp / Offer Benchmarking

Grounds an offer or client budget conversation in current market data
rather than guesswork — output is always a range with reasoning, since a
single number invites false precision.

## Required inputs

Role/title, sector, seniority, and location (comp varies enormously by all
four). Pull from a position-spec brief already in the conversation if one
exists. If a specific candidate's current comp is relevant context, note it
but don't treat it as the benchmark.

## Workflow

**A. Web search current market data.** Search for recent (this year where
possible) compensation surveys, salary reports, or comparable role postings
for this title/sector/seniority/location combination. Look for base salary
range, typical bonus/incentive structure, and equity norms where relevant
(common at growth-stage companies, less so at large corporates).

**B. Triangulate, don't anchor on one source.** Executive comp data is
noisy and heavily SEO'd — cross-check at least 2-3 sources rather than
quoting the first result. Note where sources disagree rather than picking
one silently.

**C. Contextualize.** A market range means little without framing: how does
this client's stage/size compare to the survey population, is this a
buyer's or seller's market for this skill set right now, any location-based
adjustment (remote vs HQ-based, cost-of-living differences if comparing
across cities).

## Output

A range (not a point estimate) with the reasoning behind it and sources
cited. Note explicitly that this is market guidance, not a
recommendation — final offer decisions are the client's and any
individual-specific negotiation stays with the search consultant.

## Not this skill's job

Legal or tax advice on compensation structuring, or advising on an
individual's personal financial decisions — Claude isn't a financial or
legal advisor; stick to market research.
